# VEYRA Analytical & Machine Learning Methodology

This document outlines the theoretical, mathematical, and algorithmic methodologies used in the Veyra Analytics platform across RFM segmentation, K-Means clustering, explainable churn risk scoring, and Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) estimation.

---

## 1. RFM Customer Segmentation Methodology

### Mathematical Definitions
Let $T_{\text{ref}}$ represent the analysis reference timestamp ($\max(\text{OrderDate}) + 1\text{ day}$). For customer $i$:
1. **Recency ($R_i$)**: Number of days elapsed since the customer's most recent order:
   $$R_i = T_{\text{ref}} - \max_{j \in O_i}(\text{OrderDate}_{i,j})$$
2. **Frequency ($F_i$)**: Total count of unique completed purchase orders placed by the customer:
   $$F_i = |\{ \text{OrderID}_{i,j} \}|$$
3. **Monetary ($M_i$)**: Total net historical financial revenue generated by the customer:
   $$M_i = \sum_{j \in O_i} \text{SalesAmount}_{i,j}$$

### Quintile Scoring Methodology (1–5 Scale)
Customers are ranked into 5 equal tiers (quintiles) using rank percentiles:
- **$R\text{-Score}$**: Inverted order (lower recency earns higher score $5$; dormant customers receive score $1$).
- **$F\text{-Score}$**: Direct order (higher frequency earns higher score $5$; single-order buyers receive score $1$).
- **$M\text{-Score}$**: Direct order (highest cumulative spend receives score $5$).

$$\text{RFM Score} = \text{Concat}(R\text{-Score}, F\text{-Score}, M\text{-Score})$$

### Behavioral Segment Mapping Rules
| Segment | Scoring Criteria | Strategic Business Meaning | Actionable Retention Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Champions** | $R \ge 4$, $F \ge 4$, $M \ge 4$ | Highest spend, most frequent, recent buyers | Early product previews, VIP loyalty rewards, referral advocates |
| **Loyal Customers** | $R \ge 3$, $F \ge 3$, $M \ge 3$ | Consistent repeat buyers with strong margins | Cross-sell related categories, tiered loyalty points |
| **Potential Loyalists** | $R \ge 4$, $F \in \{2, 3\}$ | Recent repeat buyers showing high velocity | Welcome back discounts, membership program invitations |
| **New Customers** | $R \ge 4$, $F = 1$ | First-time buyers with recent initial order | Onboarding email sequences, second-purchase incentive |
| **Promising** | $R = 3$, $F = 1$ | Moderate recent buyers with 1 order | Seasonal catalog showcases, product recommendations |
| **Need Attention** | $R \in \{2, 3\}$, $F \in \{2, 3\}$ | Customers slipping below average activity | Time-sensitive re-engagement offers, satisfaction surveys |
| **At Risk** | $R \le 2$, $F \ge 3$ | High historical frequency but long dormancy | Aggressive reactivation promos, personalized win-back emails |
| **Cannot Lose Them** | $R \le 2$, $F \ge 4$, $M \ge 4$ | Former top-tier VIPs now dormant | Executive outreach, substantial reactivation credits |
| **Lost Customers** | $R \le 2$, $F \le 2$ | Dormant low-frequency buyers | Minimal ad spend; low-cost automated quarterly newsletters |

## 2. K-Means Customer Clustering Methodology

### Feature Engineering
To capture multi-dimensional consumer behavior beyond RFM, we engineer 8 standardized customer attributes:
1. `Recency` (Days since last purchase)
2. `Frequency` (Total distinct orders)
3. `Monetary` (Total net revenue)
4. `AverageOrderValue` (AOV: Monetary / Frequency)
5. `TotalQuantity` (Total units bought across all orders)
6. `ReturnRate` (QuantityReturned / TotalQuantity)
7. `AverageDiscount` (TotalDiscounts / GrossValue)
8. `CustomerLifespan` (Days between first and last purchase)

### Preprocessing & Normalization
- **Logarithmic Transformation**: Skewed monetary, quantity, frequency, and lifespan distributions are transformed via $\ln(1 + x)$ to stabilize variance and achieve normal feature geometry.
- **Standard Scaling**: Normalized using `StandardScaler` to ensure zero mean ($\mu = 0$) and unit variance ($\sigma = 1$), preventing high-magnitude features (e.g., Monetary) from dominating Euclidean distance calculations.

### Cluster Evaluation ($K \in [2, 8]$)
- **Elbow Method (Inertia)**: Tracks sum of squared distances of samples to their closest cluster center:
  $$J(K) = \sum_{k=1}^K \sum_{x \in C_k} \|x - \mu_k\|^2$$
- **Silhouette Coefficient**: Measures cluster cohesion vs. separation:
  $$s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max(a(i), b(i))}$$
  where $a(i)$ is mean intra-cluster distance and $b(i)$ is nearest-cluster distance.
- **Selection of $K=5$**: Evaluated across $K=2..8$; $K=5$ corresponds to the optimal elbow bend and yields distinct, commercially actionable customer cohorts ("High-Value VIPs", "Active Steady Repeaters", "Deal Seekers & Bargain Shoppers", "Dormant Occasional Buyers", and "Mid-Tier Potential Loyalists").

## 3. Explainable Churn Risk Framework

Rather than treating churn as a black-box machine learning probability, Veyra implements an explainable behavioral framework grounded in personal customer purchase intervals.

### Inter-Purchase Cycle Formulation
For customers with multiple lifetime transactions ($F_i > 1$):
$$\Delta_{\text{avg}, i} = \frac{\text{CustomerLifespanDays}_i}{F_i - 1}$$
where $\Delta_{\text{avg}, i}$ represents the customer's historical average repurchase cadence in days.

### Churn Risk Classification
1. **Single-Order Customers ($F_i = 1$)**:
   - $\text{DaysSinceLastPurchase} \le 90\text{ days} \implies \textbf{Low Risk}$
   - $91 \le \text{DaysSinceLastPurchase} \le 270\text{ days} \implies \textbf{Medium Risk}$
   - $\text{DaysSinceLastPurchase} > 270\text{ days} \implies \textbf{High Risk}$
2. **Repeat Customers ($F_i > 1$)**:
   - $\text{DaysSinceLastPurchase} \le 1.5 \times \Delta_{\text{avg}, i} \implies \textbf{Low Risk}$
   - $1.5 \times \Delta_{\text{avg}, i} < \text{DaysSinceLastPurchase} \le 3.0 \times \Delta_{\text{avg}, i} \implies \textbf{Medium Risk}$
   - $\text{DaysSinceLastPurchase} > 3.0 \times \Delta_{\text{avg}, i}\text{ or } > 365\text{ days} \implies \textbf{High Risk}$

### Revenue at Risk
$$\text{RevenueAtRisk} = \sum_{i \in \text{High Risk}} \text{TotalRevenue}_i$$

## 4. Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) Methodology

### Formulation & Underlying Assumptions
The model combines realized historical profit with a discounted future profit velocity:
$$\text{Estimated CLV}_i = \text{HistoricalProfit}_i + \text{ProjectedFutureProfit}_i$$

$$\text{ProjectedFutureProfit}_i = \left( \frac{\text{TotalOrders}_i}{T_{\text{obs}}} \right) \times \text{AOV}_i \times \text{GrossMarginRate}_i \times M_{\text{future}}$$

### Parameters & Assumptions:
1. **$T_{\text{obs}}$ (Observation Window)**: 4.0 calendar years (2022 to 2025).
2. **Gross Margin Rate**: Customer-specific $\frac{\text{TotalProfit}_i}{\text{TotalRevenue}_i}$.
3. **Future Multiplier ($M_{\text{future}} = 2.125$)**: Assumes an expected active forward retention lifespan of 2.5 years discounted by a combined cost-of-capital and customer retention decay rate of $15\%$ annually ($2.5 \times 0.85 = 2.125$).
4. **Transparency Note**: CLV is an analytical projection for strategic cohort resource allocation, not an exact individual guarantee.
